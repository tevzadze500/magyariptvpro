Hungarian TV in Germany: the complete 2026 guide

Germany is home to the largest Hungarian community abroad: more than two hundred thousand people live and work there, from Munich through Stuttgart and Nuremberg to Berlin. The shared experience is the same: RTL+ and TV2 Play refuse to start on a German network, German cable packages carry no Hungarian channels, and the satellite dish points the wrong way. This guide covers what actually works in Germany in 2026 – from the German providers to the shared Wi-Fi of workers' accommodation.

Why doesn't Hungarian TV work in Germany?

Hungarian channels buy their programme rights for the territory of Hungary, so their platforms exclude every non-Hungarian IP address – German ones included. The EU portability regulation only helps for a temporary stay: on a two-week trip you can bring your paid Hungarian subscription. But once you are registered in Germany (Anmeldung), the provider treats you as a German resident and the regulation does not protect you.

The legal background and the country comparison are in our guide to watching Hungarian TV abroad; here we focus specifically on Germany.

Cable TV included in the rent? Not since 2024

For decades, cable TV in most German rentals was billed through the service charges (Nebenkosten) whether you watched it or not. Since 1 July 2024 landlords can no longer pass that cost on: you sign your own cable contract if you want one. That is when many Hungarian households realised they would be paying monthly for a package without a single Hungarian channel.

If you are deciding where your TV budget goes, it helps to know that the internet connection you already have is enough on its own for the full Hungarian line-up. The broadcasting fee (Rundfunkbeitrag) is owed by every household regardless – neither cable nor IPTV replaces it.

German internet: Telekom, Vodafone, O2, 1&1

You need 10-15 Mbps of stable bandwidth for HD and 25 Mbps for 4K. Even the smallest German fixed-line plan delivers that today. What to watch for:

  • DSL (Telekom, O2, 1&1): speed is steady and barely drops in the evening. The most predictable choice for television.
  • Cable internet (Vodafone): faster on paper, but shared within the building, so it can slow down between 8 and 10 pm. If the picture stutters then, drop the resolution one step.
  • Rural areas and small towns still have 16 Mbps DSL in places. That is enough for HD as long as nothing else loads the network.
  • Mobile data: German data plans are expensive and tight; an evening of HD television is 2-3 GB. Do not rely on it without an unlimited plan.

Workers' accommodation, shared flats, shared Wi-Fi: what you can do

Many Hungarian workers live in company accommodation or a shared flat (WG) where ten people share one router. The problem there is not internet speed but congested Wi-Fi. What helps in practice:

  • If your room has a wall LAN socket, connect the TV or Firestick with a cable.
  • Switch to the 5 GHz Wi-Fi network if the router offers one – it is less crowded than 2.4 GHz.
  • Set the player from HD to SD at peak time; the programme guide and catch-up still work.
  • Use 7-day catch-up: what will not play smoothly in the evening plays perfectly the next morning.

An advantage few people mention: no time difference

Germany and Hungary share a time zone. The evening news starts in the evening in Munich too, and the national team plays at the same hour as at home – no getting up at dawn like Hungarians in the UK or the US. For shift workers catch-up is still useful: after a week of nights, one press brings back what you missed.

Frequently asked questions about Hungarian TV in Germany

Do I need a VPN in Germany for Hungarian TV?

Not for IPTV: access is not tied to a Hungarian IP address, so it works the same on a German network. You would need one for RTL+ and TV2 Play, but those platforms actively block VPNs.

Do Vodafone or Telekom TV packages include Hungarian channels?

German cable and IPTV packages are built for the German market. They typically carry no Hungarian commercial channels – no RTL, no TV2 – with at most a public broadcaster or two in a separate language package.

Can I use my Hungarian subscription in Germany under the EU rule?

Temporarily, yes – for a holiday or a posting of a few weeks, EU Regulation 2017/1128 obliges paid services to keep it working. If you are registered in Germany, no: the provider verifies your member state of residence.

Is 16 Mbps DSL enough?

For HD, yes, as long as nobody else is downloading or streaming on the network. Stability matters more than speed: a steady 16 Mbps gives a better picture than a fluctuating 100 Mbps cable line at peak time.

Does it work on the shared Wi-Fi of workers' accommodation or a WG?

Yes, but at peak time a cable, the 5 GHz network or a lower resolution helps. Whatever would stutter in the evening you can watch flawlessly the next day with 7-day catch-up.

What happens if I travel home or move to another country?

Nothing. The same login works in Hungary, Austria or anywhere else, with no switching. Device limits and cancellation are covered in our FAQ.
