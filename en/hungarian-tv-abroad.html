How to watch Hungarian TV abroad: the complete 2026 guide

If you live outside Hungary, you have probably hit the wall where RTL+ or TV2 Play simply refuses to start. That is not a bug: Hungarian channels deliberately block foreign IP addresses. This guide explains why, what EU law actually allows, and which four solutions genuinely work in 2026 — broken down country by country.

Why are Hungarian channels blocked abroad?

The reason is legal, not technical. Broadcasters buy transmission rights country by country. A Hungarian contract for a film or a football match covers Hungarian territory only, so the provider is obliged to exclude everyone else.

This is enforced through geoblocking: the platform checks your IP address and refuses playback if it is not Hungarian. It is the same reason Netflix has a different catalogue in every country.

  • RTL+ — the only Hungarian platform offering live TV, but tied to a Hungarian IP. If RTL Klub specifically is what you are after, we wrote a dedicated guide: how to watch RTL Klub abroad.
  • TV2 Play — partly free, also geoblocked.
  • MTVA / M4 Sport — even more tightly restricted because of sports rights.

The EU portability regulation — and why it doesn't solve everything

People often point to the EU rule that lets you "take your subscription with you". It does exist: Regulation (EU) 2017/1128 on the cross-border portability of online content services, in force since 2018.

But it has two limits that rarely get mentioned:

  • It is mandatory only for paid services. Free platforms may opt in voluntarily — most do not.
  • It covers temporary presence only. The provider verifies your member state of residence, and if that is not Hungary, the regulation does not protect you.

In plain terms: travelling to Vienna for two weeks, you can bring your Hungarian subscription. Living abroad, the regulation does not apply to you — and you are precisely the person who needs it most.

Hungarian TV in Germany

Germany hosts the largest Hungarian community abroad, and it is where the complaint about Hungarian platforms refusing to load is most common. German bandwidth is rarely the problem: entry-level Telekom, Vodafone and O2 plans run well above 50 Mbps, which is plenty for 4K.

Watch out for one thing: many German rentals use a shared Wi-Fi that gets congested in the evening. If you see stuttering, test a wired connection first — the steps are in our troubleshooting guide.

Hungarian TV in Austria

Because Austria borders Hungary, many people try a Hungarian mobile data plan or cross-border roaming. That sometimes works right at the border, but not reliably in Vienna or Graz, and a roaming data allowance disappears fast over an evening of television.

A1 and Magenta fixed-line plans are stable, so IPTV is generally trouble-free here. If you commute between Hungary and Austria, it helps that access works identically in both countries, with no switching.

Hungarian TV in the United Kingdom

Since Brexit, the UK is outside the scope of the EU portability regulation. That is an important difference: you cannot rely on it even for a temporary stay, so bringing your Hungarian subscription is not a legal route here either.

UK broadband (BT, Virgin Media, Sky) is good quality, and Virgin's cable plans are particularly stable. In densely populated parts of London you may see evening slowdowns — switching to 5 GHz Wi-Fi or dropping the resolution one step usually fixes it.

Hungarian TV in the Netherlands and elsewhere

The Netherlands, Belgium and the Nordic countries have some of the best internet infrastructure in Europe — with KPN and Ziggo fibre plans, even 4K streams without interruption. There is essentially no technical obstacle here.

Outside the EU — Switzerland, the United States, Canada or Australia — the situation is the same, with one difference: the large time difference often puts live broadcasts at an inconvenient hour. That is where catch-up becomes genuinely important, letting you watch the last 7 days whenever suits you.

Frequently asked questions about watching Hungarian TV abroad

Can I use my Hungarian subscription elsewhere in the EU?

For temporary stays, yes: EU Regulation 2017/1128 on cross-border portability requires paid services to keep your subscription available while you travel. It does not cover permanent relocation, because providers verify your member state of residence. If you have lived in Munich for years, the regulation does not help you.

Does Hungarian TV work abroad without a VPN?

Hungarian streaming platforms filter by IP address, so playback usually fails on a foreign connection. An IPTV subscription does not rely on a Hungarian IP address, so it works without a VPN. You would only need one if your ISP throttles streaming traffic — we cover that in the troubleshooting guide.

How much internet speed do I need abroad?

For Full HD, 10-15 Mbps; for 4K, 25 Mbps of stable bandwidth is the recommended minimum. Stability matters more than raw speed: a fluctuating 50 Mbps Wi-Fi gives a worse picture than a steady 15 Mbps wired connection.

Can I watch Hungarian TV outside the EU?

Yes. IPTV is not tied to a member state, so it works the same in the UK, Switzerland, the United States, Canada or Australia. Because of the larger time difference, the catch-up function becomes especially useful there.

Is the Hungarian programme guide and catch-up available abroad?

Yes, EPG and catch-up work regardless of location, typically covering the last 7 days. Display depends on the player — TiviMate handles the guide best.

What happens when I travel back to Hungary?

Nothing. Your access works the same in Hungary, with no switching or reactivation needed. The same applies if you move to another country later.
