Hungarian TV in Switzerland 2026: Stream All Channels Without VPN

Living in Zurich, Basel, Bern, or Geneva and missing RTL, TV2, or M4 Sport? Switzerland has stricter geo-blocking than EU countries — Hungarian streaming apps are blocked immediately on Swiss networks. Here is the reliable solution.

Why is geo-blocking stricter in Switzerland than in the EU?

Many expats are surprised when paid Hungarian subscriptions (RTL+, Telekom TV GO, Vodafone TV, Direct One) or MédiaKlikk stop working as soon as they connect in Switzerland.

The reason is simple: Switzerland is outside the EU and EEA, so EU Regulation 2017/1128 on cross-border portability does not apply. On top of that, Swiss cable providers (Swisscom blue TV, Sunrise TV) do not include Hungarian commercial or sports channels in their packages.

Switzerland's Non-EU / Non-EEA Status: Why EU Portability Never Applies

If you travel temporarily to Austria or Germany with a paid Hungarian subscription such as Telekom TV GO, Yettel TV, One (Vodafone) TV, or RTL+ billed to a Hungarian address and bank card, the European Union's Cross-Border Portability Regulation (EU 2017/1128) allows temporary access. However, Switzerland is neither a member of the EU nor part of the European Economic Area (EEA), and Swiss bilateral agreements do not cover broadcast portability rights.

In practice, the moment your Smart TV, Firestick, or smartphone connects from a Swiss IP address—even if you still pay a full Hungarian subscription every month—Hungarian streaming apps are legally required to block live channels immediately. Instead of live TV, you see a regional restriction notice blocking RTL, TV2, and M4 Sport.

While some expats initially try consumer VPN services, major Hungarian platforms (especially RTL+ and MTVA MédiaKlikk) actively block commercial VPN datacenter IP ranges in 2026, while VPN encryption also reduces bandwidth and adds latency during live Formula 1 or football broadcasts. A dedicated IPTV setup engineered for Hungarian expats bypasses these headaches entirely and works natively on Swiss broadband with zero VPN needed.
