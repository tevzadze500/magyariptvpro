Hungarian TV in Austria: the complete 2026 guide

More than a hundred thousand Hungarians live and work in Austria, and tens of thousands more commute daily between Sopron, Szombathely or Mosonmagyaróvár and Vienna, Eisenstadt or Graz. The experience is the same for all of them: RTL+ and TV2 Play refuse to start on an Austrian network, and the Hungarian SIM card tricks eventually run out or get expensive. This guide covers what actually works in Austria in 2026 – with specific sections for commuters, Viennese apartment blocks and the Austrian internet providers.

Why doesn't Hungarian TV work in Austria, an EU member?

Many people assume there is no geoblocking inside the EU. That is a misunderstanding: Hungarian channels buy their programme rights for the territory of Hungary, so their platforms exclude every non-Hungarian IP address – Austrian ones included. The EU portability regulation only helps partly: for a temporary stay, such as a weekend in Vienna, you can take your paid Hungarian subscription with you. But if you are registered as a resident in Austria (Meldezettel), the provider treats you as an Austrian resident and the regulation does not protect you.

Watching with a Hungarian SIM in Austria: why it is not a lasting solution

In the border towns – between Sopron and Eisenstadt, Kőszeg and Oberpullendorf – the Hungarian mobile network often reaches across, so some people simply use a Hungarian SIM for data. That fails for three reasons:

  • Coverage: in Vienna, Graz or Linz you are already on an Austrian network, which means roaming.
  • Fair use: under the EU roaming rules, if you use the SIM more abroad than at home for four months, the operator may add surcharges.
  • Data allowance: an evening of HD television is 2-3 GB; over a month that exhausts any mobile plan.

If you still want to watch on mobile data – on the train, say – the phone setup is in our IPTV Smarters Pro guide.

Austrian internet: A1, Magenta, Drei

Austria's fixed-line network is stable. A1's fibre and copper plans, Magenta's cable internet and Drei's plans all deliver far more than the 10-15 Mbps needed for HD or the 25 Mbps for 4K. Two things deserve attention:

  • 5G home internet (Drei, Magenta): increasingly popular, and fine for television as long as the signal is stable. If you see stuttering, move the router to a window and switch to 5 GHz Wi-Fi.
  • Viennese apartment blocks: dense housing means congested 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi in the evening. A cable to the TV or a 5 GHz network fixes it – the steps are in our troubleshooting guide.

For commuters: the same access on both sides of the border

If you leave Sopron or Szombathely in the morning and come home from Vienna in the evening, only one thing matters: not having to switch anything. Your IPTV access works identically in Hungary and Austria, with the same login, on the same device. Railjet and REX trains have Wi-Fi; there, pick one resolution step lower in the phone player and the programme guide and catch-up still work.

You can use it on several devices: the Smart TV in the Vienna flat, the Firestick at home in Hungary, the phone in between. Device limits are covered in our FAQ.

Vienna, Graz, Linz, Burgenland: any difference?

Technically none – wherever there is stable internet, it works. What differs is the environment: Vienna has the largest Hungarian community in Austria, and congested building Wi-Fi is the typical issue. Graz and Linz mean detached houses or smaller blocks, with less interference. In Burgenland – around Oberwart, Unterwart and Oberpullendorf – the Hungarian mobile network sometimes still reaches, but for the reasons above it is not worth relying on.

Frequently asked questions about Hungarian TV in Austria

Do I need a VPN in Austria for Hungarian TV?

Not for IPTV: access is not tied to a Hungarian IP address, so it works the same on an Austrian network. You would need one for RTL+ and TV2 Play, but those platforms actively block VPNs, and with an Austrian address you cannot get the paid plan anyway.

Can I use my Hungarian subscription in Austria under the EU rule?

Temporarily, yes – for a holiday, a business trip or a stay of a few weeks, EU Regulation 2017/1128 obliges paid services to keep it working. If your registered residence is in Austria, no: the provider verifies your member state of residence.

Is Drei or Magenta 5G home internet enough?

Yes, if the signal is stable: 10-15 Mbps for HD, 25 Mbps for 4K. Stability matters more than speed – a fluctuating 100 Mbps 5G link gives a worse picture than a steady 20 Mbps.

Can I watch with a Hungarian SIM card in Vienna?

In Vienna you are roaming, so your data allowance and the four-month fair-use rule limit you. Right at the border the Hungarian network sometimes reaches across, but for daily television it is nowhere reliable. Use fixed-line or 5G home internet instead.

Does access keep working when I go home to Hungary for the weekend?

Yes, with no switching and the same login. This is the biggest advantage for commuters: the same access works in the Vienna flat and in the Sopron home.

Don't Austrian cable providers carry Hungarian channels?

Austrian cable and IPTV packages are built for the Austrian and German markets. They typically carry no Hungarian commercial channels – no RTL, no TV2 – with at most a public broadcaster or two in a separate language package. The full Hungarian channel list is in our FAQ.
